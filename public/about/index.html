Visual Music Player + music-connector

A music player that pictures what a song means. An LLM reads the lyrics and an audio analysis once and writes a scene script; a deterministic pixel-art engine draws it in time with the beat. A separate Mac-side service hears the music, because the LLM can't.

Hearing

music-connector decodes the song, embeds 10 s windows with MuQ-MuLan and runs librosa for tempo, beats, downbeats, band energy and novelty. Window similarity says which sections are the same music (A B B C … C).

Reading

The scene model gets the lyrics plus an Audio block: parts in time order with recurrence letters and energy, and descriptors marked as hints. It answers in a closed vocabulary, so every scene is drawable.

Drawing

compile snaps section changes to downbeats and makes returning choruses return to their world. The renderer is a pure function of time: steps land on beats, light lifts on downbeats, seeking lands on the same frame.

The contract: SongAnalysis v1

A versioned JSON Schema generated from pydantic in music-connector and mirrored in zod in the player; the player's CI fails if the two disagree on any field, type, bound or nullability. Readers accept any 1.x and ignore unknown fields. Abridged, for the first demo song:

Recommender: Thompson sampling, offline replay

music-connector v2 replaces cosine-to-medoid scoring with a Bayesian linear model per taste cluster (PCA-16 of the embeddings, partial pooling to a global posterior) and samples from it. Replayed in time order on synthetic logs (3,000 songs, 5 logs × 2,000 events, mean ± sd); the owner's own log is too small to replay, and no public dataset ships MuQ-MuLan embeddings.

Illustrated mode: FLUX on a laptop

The scene model writes one sentence per place; a local CLI renders it with FLUX.1-schnell (4-bit, MLX), cuts the sky with a depth model, splits three parallax layers, and the player re-tones them to each section's palette under its own sky, weather and sprites. Generation never runs in the browser. Measured on an M3 Pro (18 GB), no per-image cost:

Honest limits

  • Audio descriptors are hints, often wrong on vocals, era and genre; energy and tempo come from DSP, and tempo can still be 2× off.
  • The scene eval is an LLM judge on 10 songs. It can say "no measurable difference"; it can't say a script is good.
  • The recommender's eval is synthetic: it shows the model learns what it should on data shaped like a real library, not that it beats v1 on a real listener.
  • The renderer is a closed vocabulary of drawn subjects. When a lyric needs something it lacks, the script picks the nearest picture and logs the miss (vocab roadmap).
  • Illustrated plates come from heuristics on top of FLUX: a thin band of generated sky can remain, the horizon is estimated, and roads and rivers stay procedural.
  • The analysis and plate generation need Apple Silicon (or CUDA) and the MuQ-MuLan weights are non-commercial; the hosted demo uses precomputed files.
  • The demo songs' lyric timings for two songs are approximate (Whisper, corrected by hand).
